15.8Lagrange multipliers
One constraint
\(\nabla f=\lambda\nabla g,\quad g(x,y,z)=k\)
Two constraints
\(\nabla f=\lambda\nabla g+\mu\nabla h,\quad g=k,\ h=c\)
Recipe
Solve the system → list every point → evaluate \(f\) at each → largest is the max, smallest the min.
Drop λ
Two variables: \(f_x\,g_y=f_y\,g_x\), together with \(g=k\).
Region \(g\le k\)
Interior points (\(\nabla f=\mathbf 0\)) plus the boundary (Lagrange); compare all values.

✕ TrapStopping at one critical point: check every one and compare the values of \(f\). λ is not the answer.

✕ TrapDividing by \(x\) or \(y\) loses the cases \(x=0\), \(y=0\): solve those separately.

16.1–16.3Double integrals · order · polar
Type I / II
\(\displaystyle\int_a^b\!\!\int_{g_1(x)}^{g_2(x)} f\,dy\,dx\qquad \int_c^d\!\!\int_{h_1(y)}^{h_2(y)} f\,dx\,dy\)
Rectangle
\(\displaystyle\iint_{[a,b]\times[c,d]} g(x)\,h(y)\,dA=\int_a^b g\,dx\cdot\int_c^d h\,dy\)
Uses
Area \(\iint_D dA\) · volume \(\iint_D (z_{\text{top}}-z_{\text{bot}})\,dA\) · average \(\displaystyle\bar f=\frac{1}{A(D)}\iint_D f\,dA\)
Reverse order
When the inner integral has no antiderivative (\(e^{x^2},\ \sin(y^2),\ \sqrt{1+x^3}\)): sketch \(D\), read the bounds the other way.
Symmetry
\(f\) odd in \(x\), \(D\) symmetric about the \(y\)-axis \(\Rightarrow \iint_D f\,dA=0\).
Polar
\(x=r\cos\theta,\ y=r\sin\theta\) · \(x^2+y^2=r^2\) · \(dA=r\,dr\,d\theta\)
Polar curves

\(x^2+y^2=a^2\Rightarrow r=a\)

\(x^2+y^2=2ax\Rightarrow r=2a\cos\theta,\ \ -\tfrac{\pi}{2}\le\theta\le\tfrac{\pi}{2}\)

\(x^2+y^2=2ay\Rightarrow r=2a\sin\theta,\ \ 0\le\theta\le\pi\)

\(y=x\ (x>0)\Rightarrow\theta=\tfrac{\pi}{4}\) · \(x=a\Rightarrow r=a\sec\theta\) · \(y=a\Rightarrow r=a\csc\theta\)

Go polar
Disk, annulus, sector, or \(x^2+y^2\) in \(f\).

✕ TrapForgetting the \(r\) in \(dA=r\,dr\,d\theta\).

✕ TrapOuter limits must be numbers; inner limits may use only the outer variable. Sketch before reversing.

✕ Trap\(r=2a\cos\theta\) with \(0\le\theta\le2\pi\) traces the circle twice.

16.4Triple integrals
z-simple
\(\displaystyle\iiint_E f\,dV=\iint_D\left[\int_{u_1(x,y)}^{u_2(x,y)} f\,dz\right]dA\), \(D\) = shadow of \(E\) on the \(xy\)-plane
Volume
\(V=\iiint_E dV\)
Shadow
Where top meets bottom: set \(u_1(x,y)=u_2(x,y)\).
Tetrahedron
\(\tfrac xa+\tfrac yb+\tfrac zc\le1\), first octant: \(0\le z\le c\big(1-\tfrac xa-\tfrac yb\big)\), \(0\le y\le b\big(1-\tfrac xa\big)\), \(0\le x\le a\) · \(V=\tfrac{abc}{6}\)
New order
Draw the shadow on the plane of the two outer variables; the innermost runs between the two bounding surfaces.

✕ TrapA bound that uses its own variable or an inner one: each bound may use only the variables outside it.

✕ TrapTaking the shadow from \(z=0\) when the bottom surface is not \(z=0\).

16.6Mass · moments · centroid
Lamina

\(\displaystyle m=\iint_D\rho\,dA\) · \(\displaystyle M_x=\iint_D y\,\rho\,dA\) · \(\displaystyle M_y=\iint_D x\,\rho\,dA\)

\(\displaystyle(\bar x,\bar y)=\Big(\frac{M_y}{m},\frac{M_x}{m}\Big)\)

Solid

\(\displaystyle m=\iiint_E\rho\,dV\)

\(\displaystyle \bar x=\frac1m\iiint_E x\rho\,dV\) · \(\displaystyle\bar y=\frac1m\iiint_E y\rho\,dV\) · \(\displaystyle\bar z=\frac1m\iiint_E z\rho\,dV\)

Centroid
\(\rho\) constant: pure geometry. Symmetry gives coordinates for free (\(\bar x=0\) for a region symmetric about the \(y\)-axis).
Know these

Half-disk radius \(R\), \(y\ge0\): \(\bar y=\dfrac{4R}{3\pi}\) · quarter disk: \(\bar x=\bar y=\dfrac{4R}{3\pi}\)

Solid hemisphere radius \(a\), \(z\ge0\): \(\bar z=\dfrac{3a}{8}\) · solid cone height \(h\): \(\tfrac h4\) above the base

Moments of inertia
Inertia
\(I_x=\iint_D y^2\rho\,dA\) · \(I_y=\iint_D x^2\rho\,dA\) · \(I_0=I_x+I_y=\iint_D(x^2+y^2)\rho\,dA\)

✕ Trap\(M_x\) uses \(y\) (distance from the \(x\)-axis); \(M_y\) uses \(x\).

✕ TrapDividing by the area instead of the mass when \(\rho\) is not constant.

✕ TrapUsing symmetry when only the region, not the density, is symmetric.

17.1Vector fields
Gradient field
\(\mathbf F=\nabla f\): perpendicular to the level curves of \(f\), points toward larger \(f\), longer where level curves crowd.
Patterns
\(\langle x,y\rangle\) straight out · \(\langle -y,x\rangle\) counterclockwise circles · \(\langle y,-x\rangle\) clockwise
Flow line
\(\mathbf r'(t)=\mathbf F(\mathbf r(t))\)

✕ Trap\(\nabla f\) is perpendicular to level curves, never tangent.

✕ TrapA field that circulates around a loop is not a gradient field.

17.2Line integrals
Scalar
\(\displaystyle\int_C f\,ds=\int_a^b f(\mathbf r(t))\,|\mathbf r'(t)|\,dt\) · length \(\int_C ds\) · wire mass \(\int_C\rho\,ds\)
Vector (work)
\(\displaystyle\int_C\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r=\int_a^b\mathbf F(\mathbf r(t))\cdot\mathbf r'(t)\,dt\) \(\displaystyle=\int_C P\,dx+Q\,dy+R\,dz\)
dx only
\(\displaystyle\int_C f\,dx=\int_a^b f(\mathbf r(t))\,x'(t)\,dt\)
Flux (plane)
\(\displaystyle\int_C\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf n\,ds=\int_C P\,dy-Q\,dx\) (outward \(\mathbf n\), \(C\) counterclockwise)
Orientation
Reversing \(C\) flips \(\int\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r\), \(\int f\,dx\), flux. \(\int f\,ds\) does not change.
Paths

Segment \(P_0\to P_1\): \(\mathbf r(t)=(1-t)P_0+tP_1,\ 0\le t\le1\)

Circle radius \(a\), counterclockwise: \(\langle a\cos t,\,a\sin t\rangle,\ 0\le t\le2\pi\) · graph: \(\langle x,\,g(x)\rangle\)

✕ TrapForgetting the speed: \(ds=|\mathbf r'(t)|\,dt\).

✕ TrapRunning a segment over the \(x\)-range instead of \(0\le t\le1\); piecewise \(C\): add the pieces.

17.3Conservative fields
FTLI
\(\displaystyle\int_C\nabla f\cdot d\mathbf r=f(\mathbf r(b))-f(\mathbf r(a))\): end minus start; closed \(C\Rightarrow0\)
Same thing
Conservative \(\iff\) path independent \(\iff\oint_C\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r=0\) for every closed \(C\)
Test
2D: \(P_y=Q_x\) · 3D: \(\operatorname{curl}\mathbf F=\mathbf 0\) — proves conservative only on a simply connected domain (no holes, e.g. all of \(\mathbb R^2\) or \(\mathbb R^3\)).
Vortex
\(\mathbf F=\Big\langle\dfrac{-y}{x^2+y^2},\dfrac{x}{x^2+y^2}\Big\rangle\): \(P_y=Q_x\), yet \(\oint\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r=2\pi\) once counterclockwise around the origin. Not conservative.
Potential
  1. \(f=\int P\,dx+g(y,z)\)
  2. \(f_y=Q\) gives \(g\) up to \(h(z)\)
  3. \(f_z=R\) gives \(h\)
  4. Check \(\nabla f=\mathbf F\)

✕ TrapEqual cross-partials on a domain with a hole prove nothing.

✕ TrapThe constant from \(\int P\,dx\) is a function of \(y\) and \(z\), not a number.

17.4Green’s Theorem
Needs
\(C\) simple, closed, counterclockwise (\(D\) on the left); \(P,Q\) smooth on all of \(D\).
Circulation
\(\displaystyle\oint_C P\,dx+Q\,dy=\iint_D\big(Q_x-P_y\big)\,dA\)
Flux
\(\displaystyle\oint_C\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf n\,ds=\oint_C P\,dy-Q\,dx\) \(\displaystyle=\iint_D\big(P_x+Q_y\big)\,dA\)
Area
\(\displaystyle A=\oint_C x\,dy=-\oint_C y\,dx=\frac12\oint_C x\,dy-y\,dx\) · ellipse \(\pi ab\)
Hole in D
\(\oint_{\text{outer}}-\oint_{\text{inner}}=\iint_D(Q_x-P_y)\,dA\), both counterclockwise. If \(Q_x-P_y=0\) on \(D\), both loops give the same value.
Open curve
Close it with a segment \(C'\): \(\int_C=\iint_D(Q_x-P_y)\,dA-\int_{C'}\), with \(C\cup C'\) counterclockwise.
Which tool?
  • \(\operatorname{curl}\mathbf F=\mathbf 0\) on a simply connected domainpotential + FTLI
  • \(C\) closed in the plane, \(\mathbf F\) smooth insideGreen
  • \(C\) nearly closed, \(Q_x-P_y\) simpleclose it + Green
  • Anything elseparametrize

✕ TrapClockwise \(C\) flips the sign: \(-\iint_D(Q_x-P_y)\,dA\).

✕ TrapWriting \(P_y-Q_x\); it is \(Q_x-P_y\).

✕ TrapUsing Green with a singularity inside \(D\) (the vortex field around the origin): cut it out first.

17.5Divergence & curl
div (scalar)
\(\operatorname{div}\mathbf F=\nabla\cdot\mathbf F=P_x+Q_y+R_z\)
curl (vector)
\(\operatorname{curl}\mathbf F=\nabla\times\mathbf F=\big\langle R_y-Q_z,\ P_z-R_x,\ Q_x-P_y\big\rangle\)
Identities
\(\operatorname{curl}(\nabla f)=\mathbf 0\) · \(\operatorname{div}(\operatorname{curl}\mathbf F)=0\) · \(\operatorname{div}(\nabla f)=\nabla^2 f=f_{xx}+f_{yy}+f_{zz}\)
So
\(\operatorname{curl}\mathbf F\ne\mathbf 0\Rightarrow\) not a gradient · \(\operatorname{div}\mathbf F\ne0\Rightarrow\) not a curl
Makes sense?

\(\nabla\) takes a scalar \(f\); div and curl take a vector \(\mathbf F\).

✓ Defined\(\nabla f\), \(\operatorname{div}\mathbf F\), \(\operatorname{curl}\mathbf F\), \(\nabla(\operatorname{div}\mathbf F)\), \(\operatorname{div}(\nabla f)\), \(\operatorname{curl}(\operatorname{curl}\mathbf F)\)

✕ Meaningless\(\operatorname{div}f\), \(\operatorname{curl}f\), \(\nabla\mathbf F\), \(\operatorname{div}(\operatorname{div}\mathbf F)\), \(\operatorname{curl}(\operatorname{div}\mathbf F)\), \(\nabla(\operatorname{curl}\mathbf F)\)

Meaning
div \(>0\): source (net outflow), \(<0\): sink · curl: axis of a paddle wheel’s spin; in 2D \((\operatorname{curl}\mathbf F)\cdot\mathbf k=Q_x-P_y\)
Green, vector

\(\oint_C\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r=\iint_D(\operatorname{curl}\mathbf F)\cdot\mathbf k\,dA\)

\(\oint_C\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf n\,ds=\iint_D\operatorname{div}\mathbf F\,dA\)

✕ TrapThe middle component of curl is \(P_z-R_x\): the sign flips there.

✕ Trap\(\operatorname{curl}\mathbf F=\mathbf 0\) means conservative only on a simply connected domain.

17.6Surface area · surface integrals
Parametric

\(\mathbf r(u,v)\), \((u,v)\in D\): \(dS=|\mathbf r_u\times\mathbf r_v|\,du\,dv\)

\(\displaystyle\iint_S f\,dS=\iint_D f(\mathbf r(u,v))\,|\mathbf r_u\times\mathbf r_v|\,dA\)

Area
\(\displaystyle A(S)=\iint_D|\mathbf r_u\times\mathbf r_v|\,dA\)
Graph z = g
\(dS=\sqrt{1+g_x^2+g_y^2}\,dA\) over the shadow \(D\)
Sphere
\(\mathbf r=\langle a\sin\varphi\cos\theta,\ a\sin\varphi\sin\theta,\ a\cos\varphi\rangle\): \(dS=a^2\sin\varphi\,d\varphi\,d\theta\), area \(4\pi a^2\)
Cylinder
\(\mathbf r=\langle a\cos\theta,\ a\sin\theta,\ z\rangle\): \(dS=a\,d\theta\,dz\)
Cone, paraboloid
\(z=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}\): \(dS=\sqrt2\,dA\) · \(z=x^2+y^2\): \(dS=\sqrt{1+4r^2}\;r\,dr\,d\theta\)
Plane piece
Normal \(\mathbf n=\langle A,B,C\rangle\), \(C\ne0\), shadow \(D\) in the \(xy\)-plane: \(A(S)=\dfrac{|\mathbf n|}{|C|}\,A(D)\)

✕ TrapDropping the \(a^2\) in \(dS=a^2\sin\varphi\,d\varphi\,d\theta\).

✕ TrapLeaving \(f\) in \(x,y,z\): substitute the surface (\(z=g(x,y)\) or \(\mathbf r(u,v)\)) before integrating over \(D\).

✕ Trap\(|\mathbf r_u\times\mathbf r_v|\) is a length: take the magnitude of the cross product.
